---
title: Common reflux triggers and timing
source: Gastro-oesophageal reflux patient education summary
tags: [gerd, reflux, nutrition]
---

Reflux symptoms are driven by a combination of what is eaten, how much, and when. Timing is frequently more actionable than restriction.

Large meals distend the stomach and increase transient lower oesophageal sphincter relaxations. Smaller, more frequent meals are commonly better tolerated than the same total intake in two large ones.

Lying down within roughly three hours of eating is one of the most consistently reported aggravating factors. Evening meals eaten late are a common cause of overnight symptoms and of a night cough that can be mistaken for a chest problem.

Frequently reported dietary triggers include high-fat and fried foods, which slow gastric emptying; caffeine and alcohol; carbonated drinks; chocolate and peppermint; and acidic foods such as citrus and tomato. Trigger patterns are individual, and blanket elimination of all of them is rarely necessary or sustainable.

Raising the head of the bed by 15 to 20 centimetres, using blocks under the bed frame rather than extra pillows, is associated with reduced nocturnal symptoms.

Persistent symptoms despite these measures, difficulty swallowing, unintentional weight loss, or vomiting blood all warrant medical review rather than further self-management.
