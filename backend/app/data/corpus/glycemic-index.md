---
title: Glycaemic index, glycaemic load and meal composition
source: Diabetes nutrition education summary
tags: [diabetes, glycemic, nutrition]
---

Glycaemic index ranks carbohydrate-containing foods by how quickly they raise blood glucose relative to a reference. Values of 55 or below are commonly described as low, 56 to 69 as medium, and 70 or above as high.

Glycaemic index alone is an incomplete guide because it ignores portion size. Glycaemic load, which multiplies the index by the available carbohydrate in the actual serving, describes real meals better. A high-index food in a small portion can affect glucose less than a medium-index food eaten in quantity.

Meal composition changes the response. Protein, fat, and fibre eaten alongside carbohydrate slow gastric emptying and blunt the peak. The same bowl of rice produces a different curve when eaten with fish and vegetables than when eaten alone.

Order of eating has a measurable effect in several studies: consuming vegetables and protein before carbohydrate has been associated with lower post-meal glucose excursions.

Light physical activity after eating, such as a ten to fifteen minute walk, is associated with reduced post-meal glucose peaks in people with and without type 2 diabetes.

Individual responses vary substantially. Continuous glucose data from the person themselves is more informative than any published index value.
