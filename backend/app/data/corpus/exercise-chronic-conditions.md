---
title: Physical activity with chronic conditions
source: Exercise guidance summary for primary care
tags: [exercise, diabetes, hypertension, wellness]
---

General adult activity guidance suggests 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days.

For people with type 2 diabetes, activity improves insulin sensitivity, and the effect on glucose handling is short-lived enough that regular activity matters more than occasional long sessions. Gaps of more than two consecutive days are commonly discouraged. People taking insulin or sulfonylureas need individual advice about hypoglycaemia risk around exercise.

For people with raised blood pressure, regular aerobic activity is associated with meaningful average reductions. Heavy resistance work with breath-holding raises blood pressure acutely; steady breathing through the effort is generally advised.

Activity should be stopped and medical advice sought for chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or palpitations during exertion. These symptoms during activity are more concerning than the same symptoms at rest, not less.

Foot inspection before and after activity is part of routine care for people with diabetes, particularly where any loss of sensation is present.

Starting points should be built from current capacity rather than from a target. Increases of roughly ten percent per week in duration are a common conservative approach.
