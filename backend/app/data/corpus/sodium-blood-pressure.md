---
title: Dietary sodium and blood pressure
source: Public health nutrition guidance summary
tags: [hypertension, sodium, nutrition]
---

Most adults consume substantially more sodium than they need. General population guidance commonly sets a target below 2,300 mg per day, with a tighter target near 1,500 mg per day often discussed for adults with raised blood pressure. Individual targets should come from the person's own clinician.

In practice, most dietary sodium is not added at the table. It arrives in bread, processed meat, cheese, sauces, instant noodle seasoning, miso, soy sauce, and restaurant broth. A single packaged noodle serving with its broth consumed can exceed an entire day's tighter target.

Broth is a common and under-recognised source. Leaving a substantial portion of noodle or soup broth uneaten reduces sodium intake considerably without changing the meal itself.

Reductions in sodium intake are associated with modest average reductions in blood pressure, with larger effects in people whose blood pressure is already raised. Effects accumulate over weeks, not days, so single meals matter less than sustained patterns.

Potassium-rich foods such as vegetables, legumes, and fruit are generally encouraged alongside sodium reduction, though people with reduced kidney function need individual advice before increasing potassium.
